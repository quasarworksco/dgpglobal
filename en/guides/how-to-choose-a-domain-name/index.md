Your domain is what people type to reach your website, like **yourbusiness.com**. It goes on your business cards, your truck, your invoices and your email. That's why it's worth thinking it through.

## 1. Keep it short and easy to say

Imagine saying it over the phone: "your-business dot com". If you have to spell it out, it's too complicated. Ideally:

- Under 15 characters if possible.
- Words that are easy to spell in English and Spanish.
- No letters that sound alike and get mixed up.

## 2. Avoid hyphens and numbers

**garcia-cleaning-services-123.com** is hard to remember and hard to dictate. Hyphens and numbers get forgotten or mistyped. Use them only if there's no other option.

## 3. Pick the right extension

- **.com:** the best known, and the one people type by habit. It's the first choice.
- **.net or .co:** good alternatives if the .com is taken.
- **.us:** shows you're a US business.
- Local or industry extensions (.miami, .restaurant): eye-catching, but less familiar.

If you can get the .com, take it.

## 4. Your name or what you do

You have two paths:

- **Your brand:** perezconstruction.com. Builds long-term recognition.
- **Your service and city:** orlandocleaning.com. Makes it clear what you do, but it's harder to protect as a brand.

If your business name is already known, use your brand. If you're just starting, a mix of name and service usually works well.

## 5. Make sure it doesn't belong to someone else

Before buying, search the name on Google and in the trademark database. You don't want legal trouble or customers confusing you with another business.

## 6. Use it for your email too

An **info@yourbusiness.com** email looks far more professional than a Gmail address. It builds trust with every quote and invoice you send.

## 7. The domain is yours: protect it

Register it in your name (or your company's), turn on auto-renewal and keep the login safe. Many businesses lose their domain because of a forgotten renewal.
