"How much do you charge for a website?" is the question we hear most. The honest answer: **it depends on what your business needs**. A few factors decide whether a website costs a little or a lot, and knowing them helps you compare quotes without paying for things you don't need.

## 1. The type of site

This drives the price more than anything else:

- **Landing page (single page):** presents your business, your services and a contact button. It's the most affordable option and the fastest to deliver. Great if you're starting out or have one main service.
- **Multi-page site:** home, services, about, reviews and contact, each on its own page. It gives every service more room and helps you show up on Google for more searches.
- **Premium or custom site:** product catalog, booking system, online store or special features. It takes more work, so it costs more.

## 2. Who provides the content

If you already have a logo, photos and copy, the work goes faster. If you need someone to write the copy, design the logo or take photos, that adds to the price. Always ask what the quote includes.

## 3. Extra features

Every feature adds work: advanced forms, online booking, a second language, live chat, a catalog, payments. Before asking for everything, think about what you need **today** to win customers; the rest can be added later.

## 4. Hosting, domain and maintenance

A website isn't just the design. It also needs:

- **A domain:** your address, like *yourbusiness.com*, paid yearly.
- **Hosting:** where your website lives online.
- **Maintenance:** changes, updates and support when something breaks.

Some agencies include these for a period of time and others charge for them monthly. That difference changes the real cost a lot.

## 5. One-time payment or monthly plan

There are two models: you pay once for the design, or you pay a monthly fee that bundles everything. Neither is bad, but run the numbers over 2 or 3 years to compare them fairly.

## How to choose without overpaying

1. **Start with what you need to sell today.** A well-built landing page can bring in customers from week one.
2. **Ask for real examples** of sites the agency built in your industry.
3. **Ask what's included:** number of pages, copywriting, hosting, maintenance and revisions.
4. **Ask about turnaround.** A landing page shouldn't take weeks.
5. **Be wary of prices that look too cheap** if they don't include a mobile version, a contact form or support.
