When someone searches "plumber near me" or "house cleaning in Tampa", the first thing they see isn't websites: it's the **map with three businesses**. You earn that spot with a well-built Google Business Profile, and it's free.

## 1. Create or claim your profile

Go to **business.google.com** with a Google account and search for your business name. If it already shows up (Google sometimes creates it automatically), claim it. If not, create it from scratch.

## 2. Pick the right primary category

It's one of the factors that matters most. Use the category that describes **exactly** what you do: "House cleaning service" beats "Company". You can add secondary categories for your other services afterwards.

## 3. Address or service area

- If customers come to you, enter your address.
- If you go to customers (cleaning, construction, technicians), set it up as a **service-area business** and list the cities you cover. That way your home address stays hidden.

## 4. Verify your business

Google will ask you to verify it by video, phone, text or email, depending on the case. Without verification your profile won't show properly on Maps. Don't skip this step.

## 5. Complete the whole profile

The more complete it is, the more trust it earns from Google and from customers:

- Phone number and **website** (link it; it helps both).
- Business hours, including holidays.
- A list of services with a short description each.
- A business description that mentions your services and cities, written naturally.

## 6. Upload real photos

Photos of your work, your team, your vehicle or your location. Profiles with real photos get more clicks than ones with just a logo. Add new photos every month.

## 7. Get reviews (and reply to them)

Reviews are key to climbing the map. Ask every happy customer using the **direct review link** Google Business gives you. Reply to every review, including negative ones, politely and quickly. If you serve both English and Spanish speakers, getting reviews in both languages is a plus.

## 8. Post every week

Google Business lets you post updates, offers and recent work. One post a week tells Google your business is active.

## 9. Keep your details identical everywhere

Your name, phone number and address must be **exactly the same** on Google, your website, Facebook, Instagram and directories like Yelp or Bing. Inconsistencies confuse Google and cost you rankings.
