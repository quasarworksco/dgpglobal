Before visiting a restaurant, almost everyone looks it up on their phone: photos, menu, prices and directions. If they can't find that quickly, they go somewhere else. A good website is your best digital host.

## 1. The menu, visible and up to date

It's what people look for most. Put it in **text, not a PDF** or a blurry photo: it reads better on mobile and Google understands it. Include prices and highlight your most popular dishes.

## 2. A button to order or book

Depending on your business:

- **WhatsApp or one-tap call** ordering.
- Links to your delivery platforms.
- Online reservations if you have tables.

Make sure the button is visible as soon as the page opens.

## 3. Location, hours and directions

Address with a map, full hours (including holidays) and parking. If you run a **food truck**, show where you'll be each day of the week.

## 4. Photos that make people hungry

Real, well-lit photos of your dishes and your place. A good photo sells more than any description. Avoid internet photos: people notice the difference when they arrive.

## 5. Reviews front and center

Show your best Google or Yelp reviews. New customers trust what other diners say.

## 6. English and Spanish

In Florida and Texas your customers are mixed. A bilingual menu avoids confusion and attracts both audiences.

## 7. Fast on mobile

Optimized photos and a simple design. If your page is slow, a hungry customer won't wait.

## 8. Events, catering and specials

If you do catering, private events or weekly specials, give them their own section with a button to request a quote.

## 9. Connect it with Google Maps

Your Google Business Profile should link to your website and show the same hours and phone number. Read our guide [how to show up on Google Maps](/en/guides/how-to-show-up-on-google-maps/).
