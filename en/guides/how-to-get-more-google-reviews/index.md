When people search for a service on Google, they compare the businesses on the map by their stars and how many reviews they have. Reviews are also one of the factors that most affect your ranking. The good news: most happy customers **will leave a review if you ask the right way**.

## 1. Get your direct link

In your Google Business Profile, find the **"Ask for reviews"** option and copy the link. It opens the review window directly: one tap and they're in.

## 2. Ask at the right moment

The best time is **when the customer is happy**: when you finish the job, deliver the order or they thank you. If you wait a week, they've forgotten.

## 3. Ready-to-send messages

By text or WhatsApp, after finishing:

> Thank you for trusting us, Maria! If you're happy with the job, would you help us with a Google review? It means more than you'd think: [link]

By email:

> Hi John, it was great working with you. If you have 30 seconds, a Google review helps more people find us: [link]. Thank you!

In person: "Can I send you a link to leave us a review? It really helps."

## 4. Make it a habit, not a campaign

Asking for 50 reviews in one week and then nothing doesn't work as well. **Ask every customer**, every time. A few new reviews every week is ideal.

## 5. Reply to all of them

- **Positive:** thank them by name and mention the service ("Thanks, Carlos! We're glad your kitchen turned out just how you wanted").
- **Negative:** reply calmly, without arguing, and offer to resolve it privately. Future customers read how you respond.

## 6. What not to do

- **Don't buy reviews** or ask relatives to write fake ones: Google detects them and can remove your profile.
- **Don't offer money or discounts** in exchange for positive reviews: it's against Google's rules.
- **Don't only ask customers you know will give 5 stars**: ask everyone.
